# Palette options

Three directions, explored before committing. Each was contrast-tested on its text and CTA pairings (WCAG 2.1 relative luminance; script output in `BRAND.md`).

## A. Heirloom — **chosen**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Surface (60%) | Linen | `#F7F1E6` |
| Ink (30%) | Walnut ink | `#241B16` |
| Primary (10%) | Ribbon | `#7A2433` |
| Secondary | Archive green | `#2E4A3F` |
| Accent text | Brass ink | `#8C5A14` |
| Accent decorative | Brass | `#C8963E` |
| Muted | Pencil | `#66574B` |
| Success / Error | Fern / Seal red | `#2D6A3E` / `#A3261F` |

Light linen paper, dark walnut text, an oxblood ribbon for the one thing to click, brass for the details you'd find on a jewelry box. Every text pairing passes AA in light and dark (lowest: brass ink on linen, 5.21:1).

**Why it wins:** it looks like the objects. A storybook page, a gift ribbon, a brass clasp. It reads "kept for twenty years" without a single graduation cap, and it sits apart from the graduation-gift category, which is royal blue, gold foil, and confetti.

## B. Playful premium

| Surface | Ink | Primary | Secondary | Accent | Muted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#FFF7EC` cream | `#14213D` navy | `#E2553F` coral | `#1F6F78` sea | `#F2C14E` marigold | `#55607A` |

Energetic and kid-forward. **Fails:** coral CTA with white text is 3.75:1, and coral on cream is 3.53:1. Both are large-text only. Darkening the coral to pass turns it brick-red and the direction loses its point. It also skews toward the kid rather than the parent who's buying a 20-year object.

## C. Modern archive

| Surface | Ink / Primary | Secondary | Accent | Muted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#ECEAE4` stone | `#1C1C1A` graphite | `#5B6355` sage | `#C2410C` signal orange | `#5F5E58` |

Museum-catalog cool. **Fails:** signal orange on stone is 4.30:1, below AA for body text. More important, it's cold. It reads like a gallery gift shop, not a family heirloom, and it shares a look with many DTC brands.

## Decision

Heirloom. It's the only direction that passes AA on every pairing without compromise, and the only one where the palette itself says "keepsake".
